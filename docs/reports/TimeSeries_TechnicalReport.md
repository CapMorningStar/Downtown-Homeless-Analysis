## Executive summary

The nine Assignment 4 time series require different treatment before forecasting. At their reported levels, none of the methodology-adjusted `total_count` series satisfies both stationarity tests: City Center and Marina are classified as nonstationary by both tests, while the other seven produce mixed ADF and KPSS evidence. The corresponding `raw_count` sensitivity series produces the same two-versus-seven classification pattern, indicating that the result is not created solely by the methodology multipliers embedded in `total_count`.

First differencing is the minimum successful treatment for the two aggregate series and six continuously reported neighborhoods. Outside Perimeter, which begins only in 2021, reaches joint stationarity after a `log1p` transformation without differencing. Every selected series passes the combined rule of ADF p < 0.05 and KPSS p > 0.05. These calls remain unchanged when the series are truncated before the first unreleased 2025 month, reducing concern that the four in-memory interpolations drive the conclusions.

Autocorrelation remains material for several stationary series. The reported-footprint aggregate and East Village have no Ljung-Box evidence of remaining serial dependence through 24 months, while the fixed-core-six aggregate, City Center, Columbia, Marina, Gaslamp, and Outside Perimeter do. The ACF and PACF screens therefore provide useful ARIMA starting orders, but they do not select final models. Task 7 should compare neighboring specifications with rolling-origin validation and residual diagnostics.

## Data scope and analytical design

The analysis uses `data/MasterCounts_Adjusted_v2.csv`, restricted to `component == "total"`, `area_type == "neighborhood"`, and the seven requested neighborhoods: East Village, City Center, Columbia, Marina, Cortez, Gaslamp, and Outside Perimeter. Two aggregates are added. The reported-footprint total sums every neighborhood available in each month. The fixed-core-six total excludes Outside Perimeter to preserve a stable geographic footprint over the full 2017-2025 analysis window.

The same missing-data policy used in Tasks 4.1-4.3 is retained. Four unreleased months in 2025 are interpolated only in memory to maintain a regular monthly grid. Structural absence before Outside Perimeter reporting began is not imputed. The forecasting export identifies directly interpolated rows and any differenced value that depends on an interpolated observation.

Two related meanings of “raw and adjusted” are kept separate. The public-facing methodology-adjusted measure is `total_count`; `raw_count` is tested as a methodology sensitivity. A second adjustment stage then applies transformations or differencing to `total_count` for forecasting. This distinction prevents a transformed forecasting series from being confused with the source dataset’s raw-count field.

## Stationarity tests and transformation rule

The Augmented Dickey-Fuller test evaluates the null hypothesis that a unit root is present. The KPSS test evaluates the null hypothesis that the series is stationary. A result is called consensus stationary only when ADF rejects its null at 5 percent and KPSS does not reject its null at 5 percent. Tests use ADF lag selection by AIC and automatic KPSS bandwidth selection. Constant and constant-plus-trend level specifications are retained in `StationarityTests.csv`; transformation selection uses the constant specification appropriate to a mean-stationary ARIMA input.

Candidate treatments are evaluated in a fixed order that minimizes information loss: level, `log1p` level, first difference, `log1p` first difference, seasonal difference at lag 12, combined ordinary and seasonal difference, and second difference variants. The first candidate reaching ADF/KPSS consensus is selected. This rule avoids discretionary treatment changes after viewing the preferred result.

## Autocorrelation structure and model implications

ACF and PACF plots were generated through 36 monthly lags where sample length permits, with approximate 95 percent confidence bands and reference lines at lags 12 and 24. Initial nonseasonal orders use the consecutive significant-lag pattern, capped at three to avoid translating noisy distant spikes into unnecessarily complex models. Seasonal AR or MA terms are suggested only when the corresponding PACF or ACF coefficient at lag 12 exceeds the confidence bound.

The reported-footprint aggregate is the simplest case: first differencing removes the dominant persistence, no early ACF/PACF cutoff suggests a required AR or MA term, and the Ljung-Box tests do not reject white noise. This makes ARIMA(0,1,0) a defensible benchmark, not an automatic final model. The fixed-core-six series retains dependence and should begin with ARIMA(1,1,1) alongside simpler neighboring alternatives.

East Village and Cortez also show limited residual dependence after first differencing, screening as ARIMA(2,1,1) and ARIMA(1,1,1) respectively. City Center, Columbia, Marina (screened as ARIMA(1,1,1)), and Gaslamp retain stronger serial structure and require AR/MA terms or richer intervention handling. Outside Perimeter exhibits persistent dependence even after its stationarity transformation; the suggested ARMA(1,2) structure must be treated cautiously because only 60 monthly observations are available.

No series receives an automatic seasonal AR or MA term from the lag-12 screen. This agrees with Task 4.2’s finding that recurring calendar-month effects are modest and series-specific. Seasonal alternatives should still be included as challengers during Task 7 validation rather than excluded solely from these plots.

## Robustness, limitations, and forecasting handoff

All nine selected transformations retain consensus-stationary classifications when each series is truncated before the first unreleased 2025 month. This sensitivity supports the in-memory interpolation policy but does not turn interpolated months into observations. Six transformed rows per full-history first-differenced series depend directly or indirectly on an interpolated value; Outside Perimeter has four directly interpolated rows because it is not differenced. These flags must remain available to Task 7 for exclusion or sensitivity scoring.

ADF and KPSS tests have limited power in short or structurally unstable samples. The methodology changes and COVID-era disruption documented in Task 4.3 can cause apparent persistence or rejection even when a single differencing order is operationally useful. Likewise, Ljung-Box rejection indicates remaining dependence but does not identify a unique ARIMA structure. Structural-break indicators, alternative geographic footprints, and intervention terms should be evaluated during forecasting.

`data/AdjustedTimeSeries_Forecasting.csv` is the canonical handoff. It contains the original adjusted value, final stationary value, transformation metadata, ordinary and seasonal differencing orders, source and interpolation-dependency flags, final ADF/KPSS p-values, and screened ARIMA orders. Forecasts must be inverted using the recorded transformation and the last observed level; predictions on differenced or `log1p` scales are not directly interpretable as monthly population counts.

The complete reproducible record is provided by `Stationarity_Autocorrelation.ipynb`, `data/StationarityTests.csv`, `data/AutocorrelationDiagnostics.csv`, the nine saved ACF/PACF figures, and `data/AdjustedTimeSeries_Forecasting.csv`. Task 7 should treat the order suggestions as candidate neighborhoods, compare them with rolling-origin cross-validation, and accept a final model only after residuals are approximately white noise and forecast performance improves on transparent baselines.
